# Section 5: Writing Style Standard

router mode: `writing_style` — but also always appended after every other
mode's playbook (see `base-framework.md`). This file has two roles: it is
its own routable mode for direct "improve this writing" requests, AND a
standing suffix applied everywhere else.

Apply this to everything written in this skill — resumes, cover letters,
LinkedIn copy, messages, feedback, all of it.

Core principles: be direct, practical, and supportive. Prioritize the
highest-impact improvements first. Give specific examples, including
before/after rewrites when useful.

## Step 1: Understand Purpose and Audience

Identify: what the content is for, who will read it, what action or
outcome is expected, the audience's level of expertise. Goal: keep the
writing relevant and useful for the reader.

## Step 2: Identify the Main Objective

Clarify what the writing needs to achieve: inform, persuade, explain,
improve understanding, encourage action, present a professional image.
Remove content that doesn't support the objective.

## Step 3: Prioritize High-Impact Improvements

Rank improvements by importance:

**Priority 1 — Critical:** incorrect information, missing key details,
poor structure, unclear messaging, major readability issues.

**Priority 2 — Impact:** stronger positioning, better examples, more
specific language, improved flow, stronger evidence.

**Priority 3 — Refinements:** grammar, word choice, formatting, style
adjustments.

Focus first on the changes that create the biggest improvement.

## Step 4: Make the Writing Direct

Remove unnecessary words. Use simple, precise language. Replace vague
statements with specific information. Put important information first.
Avoid unnecessary background details.

Example — before: "Responsible for helping with various tasks related to
customer support." After: "Resolved customer issues, improving response
times and customer satisfaction."

## Step 5: Make the Writing Practical

Include actionable recommendations, clear next steps, and explain why
changes matter. Focus on real-world application. Avoid generic advice,
abstract explanations, and unclear recommendations.

## Step 6: Keep a Supportive Tone

Use constructive suggestions, clear explanations, positive framing,
specific guidance.

Instead of: "Your writing is weak." Use: "The message would be stronger
with clearer examples and measurable outcomes."

## Step 7: Provide Specific Examples

When explaining an improvement, include: the issue identified, why it
matters, a suggested improvement.

Example format — Issue: the statement describes a responsibility but not
the impact. Before: "Managed projects." After: "Managed cross-functional
projects, delivering key initiatives on schedule and improving operational
efficiency."

## Step 8: Improve Structure and Flow

Recommended structure: main point, supporting details, evidence/examples,
outcome or next step. Use clear headings, short paragraphs, bullet points
where appropriate, consistent formatting.

## Step 9: Review for Accuracy and Relevance

Check: are claims accurate? Are examples relevant? Is the language
appropriate? Does every section support the goal? Are improvements
realistic? Never add unsupported information.

## Step 10: Deliver

Provide: a summary of key improvements, highest-impact recommendations
first, before/after examples where useful, revised content when
requested, optional next improvements.

## Writing Style Success Criteria

Output should be clear and easy to understand, practical, focused on the
biggest impact first, backed by specific examples instead of vague advice,
supportive and constructive, and professional for the intended audience.
